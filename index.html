Healthcare Scheduling Challenges

Medical Test Scheduling Can Become Complex

Diagnostic facilities operate under tight resource bottlenecks. Coordinating varied test protocols, specialized rooms, patient transport, and emergency priorities manually creates friction and equipment downtime.

Multiple Diagnostic Rooms

MRIs, CT scanners, sonography, and X-ray suites have dedicated hardware requirements and cannot be swapped arbitrarily.

Different Test Durations

Scans range from 15-minute radiographs to 60-minute multi-sequence spine MRIs, complicating fixed calendar grids.

Priority Patients

STAT and critical clinical cases require immediate allocation without disrupting routine outpatient appointments.

Patient Availability

Patients arrive with limited time windows, transport dependencies, and mandatory prep/fasting restrictions.

Room Conflicts

Inadvertent overlaps waste expensive radiological equipment time and cause patient waiting room congestion.

Double Bookings

Decentralized receptionist booking channels frequently trigger double-booked scanner slots.

Changing Schedules

Emergency cases and procedure overruns cascade into day-long scheduling chaos if not dynamically managed.

Manual Coordination

Spreadsheets, paper logs, and disparate phone messages consume hours of administrative staff time every day.

Hackathon / Technical Dossier

Project & Algorithm Overview

Comprehensive technical breakdown aligned with standard problem-solving documentation.

1. Problem Statement & Objectives

In diagnostic healthcare facilities, medical imaging equipment represents major capital expenditures whose under-utilization or schedule congestion degrades patient outcomes and increases operating costs. The scheduling challenge requires assigning patient diagnostic tests \(T\) into available time intervals \([S, E)\) across rooms \(R\) while adhering to:

  • Equipment Compatibility: Test \(T_i\) can only execute in compliant room \(R_k \in Compatible(T_i)\).
  • Duration Constraints: The assigned slot interval must satisfy \(E_i - S_i \ge Duration(T_i)\).
  • Non-Overlapping Invariant: For any two appointments \(i, j\) assigned to the same room \(R_k\), their time intervals must not conflict: \(\neg (S_i < E_j \land S_j < E_i)\).
  • Patient Availability: The scheduled interval must fall entirely within the patient's arrival and departure bounds \([AvailFrom_i, AvailUntil_i]\).
  • Clinical Priority Hierarchy: STAT/Urgent requests must take precedence over routine monitoring.

2. Proposed Algorithmic Approaches

The project demonstrates 6 foundational paradigms mapped to their natural clinical operations:

  • Greedy: Immediate online assignment of incoming outpatient bookings. Sorts queue by priority weight and assigns the earliest compatible non-conflicting slot.
  • Divide & Conquer: Large hospital networks partition scheduling problems hierarchically: first by Date, then by Diagnostic Modality (MRI, CT, Ultrasound). Partitions are scheduled concurrently and unified.
  • Dynamic Programming: Weighted interval optimization. Solves \(OPT(j) = \max(w_j + OPT(p(j)), OPT(j-1))\) to maximize room utilization and minimize idle gaps.
  • Backtracking: Recursive constraint satisfaction for tightly coupled multi-procedure bookings (e.g. contrast CT followed immediately by ultrasound), with systematic rollbacks upon conflict.
  • Branch & Bound: Evaluates permutations of multi-room schedules, pruning subtrees whose lower-bound cost exceeds the known best feasible schedule.
  • Graph Representation: Models tests as vertices, room affinity as bipartite edges, and overlapping requests as conflict edges (Interval Graph coloring to compute minimum rooms).

3. Algorithmic Complexity Comparison

Algorithm Paradigm Best Case Average Case Worst Case Space Complexity
Greedy Allocation O(N log N) O(N log N + N · M) O(N · M · S) O(N + M)
Divide & Conquer O(K · (N/K log(N/K))) O(N log N) O(N log N) O(N)
Dynamic Programming O(N log N) O(N log N) O(N²) O(N)
Backtracking O(N · M) O(M^N with pruning) O(M^N) O(N) stack
Branch & Bound O(N log N) O(B^d pruned) O(M^N) O(B · d) PQ
Interval Graph O(V + E) O(V + E) O(V · E) O(V + E)
Where \(N\) = number of appointments, \(M\) = rooms, \(S\) = 15-min intervals, \(V\) = vertices, \(E\) = conflict edges.

4. Implementation & Results

The frontend demo implements deterministic constraint checking in 15-minute discrete increments across 5 simulated diagnostic suites (2 MRI, 1 CT, 1 Ultrasound, 1 X-Ray). Test cases verified:

  • Overlapping Conflict Test: Verified that an MRI request between 09:00 - 09:45 in MRI Room 1 is correctly rejected with detailed conflict explanation when occupied.
  • Duration Fitting: Verified that 60-minute tests correctly reject 30-minute free gaps.
  • Priority Reordering: Confirmed that URGENT priority requests claim earliest available morning slots.

5. Future Scope & References

Future extensions include bidirectional HL7/FHIR EHR synchronization, machine learning-driven patient no-show likelihood modeling, and reinforcement learning for dynamic slot overbooking policies.
